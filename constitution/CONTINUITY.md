# Section 0 — Declaration

Continuity is the preservation of reconstructable truth across time.

Continuity is the primary purpose of PING.

Continuity is more fundamental than software.

Continuity is more fundamental than infrastructure.

Continuity is more fundamental than implementations.

Continuity exists to ensure that truth survives change.

---

# Section 1 — Definition

A system possesses continuity when it can reconstruct reality despite the loss of the systems that originally recorded it.

Continuity is not storage.

Continuity is not backup.

Continuity is not replication.

Continuity is not persistence.

Continuity is reconstructability.

A continuity-preserving system must be capable of answering:

* What happened?
* Why did it happen?
* Who authorized it?
* What evidence exists?
* How can it be reconstructed?

A system that cannot answer these questions has experienced continuity degradation.

---

# Section 2 — Continuity Objects

Continuity applies to all constitutional artifacts.

Examples include:

* events
* witnesses
* identities
* lineage
* observations
* claims
* evidence
* knowledge
* decisions
* governance artifacts
* capabilities
* archives

The loss of any artifact may be acceptable.

The loss of reconstructability is not.

---

# Section 3 — Continuity Requirements

A continuity-preserving system must satisfy the following requirements.

Reconstructability

Truth must be reconstructable.

Verifiability

Truth must be independently verifiable.

Traceability

Artifacts must preserve lineage.

Explainability

Artifacts must remain explainable.

Governability

Authorized actions must remain auditable.

Portability

Truth must survive implementation replacement.

Survivability

Truth must survive platform replacement.

---

# Section 4 — Continuity Failure

Continuity failure occurs when reconstructable truth is lost.

Examples include:

* missing lineage
* missing evidence
* unrecoverable history
* hidden authorities
* non-replayable state
* unverifiable decisions
* undocumented capabilities

Continuity failure does not require data loss.

A system may retain data while losing continuity.

State without explanation is continuity failure.

Knowledge without lineage is continuity failure.

Governance without history is continuity failure.

---

# Section 5 — Institutional Forgetting

Institutional Forgetting is the primary adversary of continuity.

Institutional Forgetting is the loss of reconstructable:

* context
* knowledge
* decisions
* governance history
* capability history
* organizational memory

Institutional Forgetting produces:

* governance instability
* knowledge degradation
* architectural drift
* capability erosion
* organizational confusion

Institutions fail because they forget.

Continuity exists to resist forgetting.

---

# Section 6 — Continuity and Truth

Truth and continuity are not identical.

Truth defines reality.

Continuity preserves the ability to reconstruct reality.

Truth may exist without continuity.

Lost truth cannot be reconstructed.

Continuity exists to prevent truth from becoming unrecoverable.

The purpose of continuity infrastructure is not to create truth.

The purpose of continuity infrastructure is to preserve truth.

---

# Section 7 — Continuity and Knowledge

Knowledge depends upon continuity.

Knowledge without continuity eventually becomes opinion.

Knowledge without evidence eventually becomes assertion.

Knowledge without lineage eventually becomes folklore.

Knowledge without replay eventually becomes interpretation.

Continuity allows knowledge to remain trustworthy.

---

# Section 8 — Continuity and Governance

Governance depends upon continuity.

Governance requires the ability to reconstruct:

* decisions
* authorities
* responsibilities
* authorizations
* outcomes

Governance that cannot explain itself is continuity failure.

Governance history is a continuity artifact.

---

# Section 9 — Continuity and Capability

Capabilities change over time.

Authorities change over time.

Permissions change over time.

Continuity requires preserving the history of those changes.

A system must be capable of reconstructing:

* who possessed authority
* when authority existed
* why authority existed
* how authority changed

Capability history is a continuity artifact.

---

# Section 10 — Continuity and Archives

Archives exist to preserve continuity.

Archives are survivability mechanisms.

Archives are not authoritative truth.

Archives preserve the artifacts required to reconstruct truth.

An archive succeeds when continuity survives implementation replacement.

---

# Section 11 — Continuity and Sovereignty

A sovereign node preserves continuity independently.

A sovereign node must be capable of:

* storing continuity artifacts
* verifying witnesses
* reconstructing history
* rebuilding knowledge
* exporting archives

Sovereignty exists to reduce continuity dependence on external systems.
