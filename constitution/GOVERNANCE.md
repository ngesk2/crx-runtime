---

# Section 0 — Declaration

Governance exists to preserve continuity.

Governance does not create truth.

Governance operates upon truth.

Truth constrains governance.

Governance may authorize actions.

Governance may not redefine reality.

Governance exists to coordinate legitimate change while preserving continuity.

---

# Section 1 — Definition

Governance is the system by which authority is created, exercised, delegated, constrained, reviewed, and revoked.

Governance answers:

* Who may act?
* What actions are permitted?
* Under what conditions?
* With what accountability?
* Through which procedures?

Governance exists to make authority explicit.

Hidden authority constitutes governance failure.

---

# Section 2 — Authority

Authority is the legitimate ability to influence constitutional artifacts.

Authority may include:

* creation authority
* modification authority
* approval authority
* review authority
* delegation authority
* revocation authority

Authority is not truth.

Authority does not establish reality.

Authority establishes permission.

All authority must be reconstructable.

---

# Section 3 — Legitimacy

Authority is legitimate only when:

* explicitly granted
* traceable
* reconstructable
* reviewable
* revocable

Untraceable authority is illegitimate.

Hidden authority is illegitimate.

Irreconstructable authority is illegitimate.

Authority must derive from constitutional processes.

---

# Section 4 — Governance Hierarchy

Governance operates under constitutional hierarchy.

Constitution
↓
Governance
↓
Capability
↓
Decision
↓
Execution

Authority flows downward.

Legitimacy flows downward.

Interpretation does not flow upward.

No lower layer may override a higher constitutional layer.

---

# Section 5 — Governance Responsibilities

Governance must:

* preserve continuity
* preserve legitimacy
* preserve accountability
* preserve reconstructability
* preserve explainability

Governance must never sacrifice continuity for convenience.

Governance must never sacrifice legitimacy for efficiency.

---

# Section 6 — Accountability

Every exercise of authority must answer:

* Who acted?
* What authority existed?
* Why was the action permitted?
* Which governance process authorized it?
* When did authorization occur?

Authority without accountability constitutes governance failure.

Governance actions must remain reconstructable.

---

# Section 7 — Delegation

Authority may be delegated.

Responsibility may not be delegated completely.

Delegation creates additional lineage.

Delegation must remain reconstructable.

Delegation must answer:

* Who delegated?
* What authority was delegated?
* To whom?
* Under which conditions?
* For what duration?

Delegation without lineage constitutes governance failure.

---

# Section 8 — Revocation

Authority must be revocable.

Delegated authority must be revocable.

Capabilities must be revocable.

Revocation must be reconstructable.

A governance system incapable of revocation is constitutionally incomplete.

---

# Section 9 — Decisions

Decisions are governance artifacts.

Decisions select among alternatives.

Decisions must reference:

* evidence
* knowledge
* responsible authorities

Decisions must preserve lineage.

Decisions must remain replayable.

Decisions are not truth.

Decisions are governance interpretations of truth.

---

# Section 10 — Governance and Knowledge

Governance depends upon knowledge.

Knowledge does not depend upon governance.

Governance may evaluate knowledge.

Governance may not redefine knowledge.

Governance may authorize action based upon knowledge.

Governance may not alter reality through declaration.

Truth constrains governance.

Governance does not constrain truth.

---

# Section 11 — Governance and Capability

Capabilities are governance artifacts.

Governance creates capabilities.

Governance modifies capabilities.

Governance revokes capabilities.

Capabilities define what actions are legal.

Capabilities do not define what is true.

Authority operates through capabilities.

---

# Section 12 — Governance and Sovereignty

Sovereign nodes govern themselves.

Sovereignty does not exempt nodes from constitutional constraints.

A sovereign node may:

* establish local governance
* establish local capabilities
* establish local authorities

provided constitutional principles remain preserved.

Governance compatibility is preferred over governance uniformity.

---

# Section 13 — Governance Survivability

Governance must survive:

* leadership replacement
* organizational replacement
* implementation replacement
* platform replacement
* infrastructure replacement

Governance survives when authority remains reconstructable.

Governance survives when decisions remain explainable.

Governance survives when capability history remains preserved.

Governance survives when legitimacy remains verifiable.

Constitutions must evolve.

Governance exists to facilitate legitimate evolution.

# Section 15 — Governance Failure

Governance failure occurs when authority becomes:

* hidden
* unreconstructable
* unverifiable
* unaccountable
* irrevocable

Examples include:

* undocumented authority
* unexplained decisions
* irreversible delegation
* lost governance history
* capability drift

Governance failure may occur without organizational collapse.

Governance failure begins when legitimacy can no longer be reconstructed.

---

# Section 16 — Long-Term Governance Principle

Governance systems change.

Leaders change.

Organizations change.

Institutions change.

Authority structures change.

Constitutional legitimacy must survive those changes.

The purpose of governance is not control.

The purpose of governance is the preservation of legitimate authority.

---

# Section 17 — Final Principle

Truth constrains governance.

Governance authorizes action.

Authority requires legitimacy.

Legitimacy requires accountability.

Accountability requires reconstructability.

Reconstructability requires continuity.

Governance exists to ensure that authority remains legitimate across time.
