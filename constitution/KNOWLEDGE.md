## Section 1 — Constitutional Primitives

### Constitution

The highest authority.
Defines legality.
Defines governance.
Defines authority boundaries.
No lower layer may override the Constitution.

### Event

The constitutional unit of reality.

**Definition:**
An immutable occurrence.

**Constitutional Law:**
Nothing exists without an event.
Events are the deepest constitutional primitive.
All constitutional artifacts ultimately derive from events.

### Witness

Cryptographic proof that an event occurred.
Witnesses establish verifiability.
Witnesses do not establish interpretation.
Witnesses prove existence.

### Replay

Deterministic reconstruction of reality from events.
Replay establishes continuity.
Replay reconstructs state.
Replay does not reinterpret state.

### Identity

A constitutionally assigned reference to:
- actors
- events
- artifacts
- capabilities
- archives
- nodes

Only authorized identity authorities may issue identities.
Identity enables continuity across time.

### Lineage

The reconstructable chain linking an artifact to its origin.
Lineage must survive transformations.
Loss of lineage constitutes continuity degradation.
Lineage enables explainability.

### Observation

A normalized representation of captured reality.
Observations derive from events.
Observations are not interpretations.
Observations are structured descriptions of reality.

### Claim

A proposition derived from observations.
Claims require evidence.
Claims are not knowledge.
Claims may be true or false.

### Evidence

Artifacts supporting or refuting claims.
Evidence must maintain lineage.
Evidence must remain replayable.
Evidence is verifiable support.

### Knowledge

Interpretation derived from replayable observations, claims, and evidence.
Knowledge is derived.
Knowledge is not authority.
Knowledge may evolve while underlying events remain unchanged.

### Decision

A governance artifact selecting among alternatives.
Decisions must reference:
- knowledge
- evidence
- responsible authority

Decisions must remain replayable.

### Capability

A constitutionally authorized action.
Capabilities define what actions are legal.
Capabilities are governance artifacts.
Capabilities do not establish truth.
Capabilities establish permission.

### Projection

A derived representation of constitutional artifacts.

**Examples:**
- vector indexes
- knowledge graphs
- dashboards
- search indexes
- caches
- presentations
- reports

Projections may be destroyed and rebuilt
without loss of constitutional truth.

Projections are never authority.

Projections may not introduce knowledge
that cannot be reconstructed from
constitutional artifacts.

Constitutional truth must remain derivable
from events, witnesses, replay, lineage,
evidence, and knowledge artifacts.

Projection loss must not result in
continuity loss.

### Continuity

The ability to preserve and reconstruct truth across time.
Continuity is the primary objective of the platform.

### Sovereignty

The ability for a node to preserve continuity without dependence on external authority.

A sovereign node must be capable of:
- storing events
- verifying witnesses
- replaying history
- reconstructing knowledge
- exporting archives

## Section 4 — Constitutional Laws

### Law 1 — Never Trust Mutable State

**Description**
State must never be treated as authoritative history.

**Rationale**
State changes.
History does not.

**Consequence**
All authoritative state must be replayable from events.

### Law 2 — Never Trust Model Output

**Description**
Models may propose.
Models may not establish truth.

**Rationale**
Models change.
Truth must survive model replacement.

**Consequence**
All model-generated artifacts require evidence and lineage.

### Law 3 — Never Bypass Lineage

**Description**
Every artifact must answer:
Where did this come from?

**Consequence**
All constitutional artifacts require provenance.

### Law 4 — Never Create Hidden Authority

**Description**
Authority must be explicit.

**Consequence**
Agents, applications, databases, caches, and models may not become authority accidentally.

### Law 5 — Never Allow Knowledge Without Replay

**Description**
Knowledge must be reconstructable.

**Consequence**
Knowledge without replay is not constitutional knowledge.

### Law 6 — Every Artifact Must Be Explainable

**Description**
Every artifact must be capable of answering:
- Who created me?
- What created me?
- Why was I created?
- Which events created me?
- Which authority allowed me?

**Consequence**
Artifacts that cannot answer these questions are constitutionally incomplete.

### CONSTITUTIONAL REVIEW NOTE K-001

The placement of Capability below Decision is intentional for v1.0.

This hierarchy represents constitutional ontology, not execution order.

Events remain the deepest constitutional primitive.

Capabilities are governance artifacts created, assigned, modified, revoked, and exercised through constitutional processes that themselves generate events.

Future constitutional review may evaluate an alternative authority hierarchy:

```

↓

↓
Event
↓
...
```

Until such amendment is ratified, the current hierarchy remains authoritative.
